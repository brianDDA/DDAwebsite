New Jersey Insurance Brief

Updated October 22, 2026

Practical updates for New Jersey property owners, drivers, employers, taxi operators, and school transportation contractors.

Quick take: NJDOBI has secured more than $10.5 million in GEICO refunds and credits. Report flood losses promptly, prepare for 2027 wage changes, and keep employer and fleet-safety procedures current.

For Property Owners

Flood Claims Do Not Require A Disaster Declaration

Homeowners and business owners with flood insurance should report flood damage to their insurer promptly. NFIP claims do not require a disaster declaration; disaster assistance is a separate process. Claim payments depend on the coverage in force at the time of the loss and the documentation provided.

What to do: Enter only when safe, photograph or video the damage, keep receipts and an inventory of losses, and follow your insurer's instructions. After documenting damage, discard items that pose health risks and take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. Consult your agent or adjuster before signing repair or cleanup contracts.

For New Jersey Drivers

New Jersey Recovers Over $10.5 Million For GEICO Customers

On October 20, 2026, NJDOBI announced more than $10.5 million in refunds and credits following an examination that found improper practices affecting more than 15,000 GEICO policyholders. GEICO and its affiliates also agreed to a $1.2 million penalty. Refunds began during the examination as errors were identified.

NJDOBI cited four violations:

  • Premium overcharges: Rates exceeded the amounts approved by NJDOBI.
  • Improper cancellations: Policies were canceled for reasons the department found improper.
  • Missing notice language: Notices that could lead to nonrenewal omitted required wording.
  • Missing total-loss disclosures: Customers keeping a totaled vehicle after settlement were not informed of their rights.

What to do: Ask GEICO whether your policy was affected and whether a refund or credit has been issued. Keep billing records and policy notices. For unresolved concerns, request assistance or file a complaint with NJDOBI.

Additional reporting: News 12 New Jersey, October 21, 2026.

2026 Automobile Liability Minimums Are Now In Effect

For standard New Jersey automobile policies issued or renewed on or after January 1, 2026, minimum bodily injury liability limits increased to $35,000 per person and $70,000 per accident. The minimum property damage limit remains $25,000. The State bulletin notes that these increases do not apply to New Jersey's Basic Policy.

What to do: Review your declarations page at renewal and consider whether the legal minimum provides enough protection for your household and assets.

School's Back: Slow Down In School Zones

The New Jersey Department of Labor launched a statewide safety campaign after three school crossing guards were killed and nine were injured during the past three years. Drivers are urged to obey reduced speed limits, put phones away, stop completely for crossing guards, never pass a stopped school bus, and watch carefully for children walking or bicycling.

What to do: Leave extra time, eliminate distractions, and be prepared to stop near schools, buses, crosswalks, and crossing guards. School transportation operators should reinforce these reminders with every driver.

For Businesses and Fleets

Operation Safe Driver Results Reinforce Fleet Safety Basics

On October 5, 2026, the Commercial Vehicle Safety Alliance released results from Operation Safe Driver Week, held July 12–18 across North America. Officers issued 3,968 warnings and 2,604 citations to commercial and passenger-vehicle drivers. Speeding was the most frequently cited unsafe driving behavior; seat-belt violations and texting or handheld-device use were also prominent. These are campaign totals, not New Jersey-only results or a new insurance requirement.

What to do: Taxi, livery, school transportation, vanpool, and other fleet operators should reinforce speed limits, seat-belt use, distraction prevention, and safe following distances. Review driver records and available telematics, and document training and coaching.

School Transportation Contractors: Confirm Insurance and Certificates

The New Jersey Department of Education requires anyone providing or subcontracting student transportation to maintain at least $1.5 million in automobile liability coverage for bodily injury and property damage. Certificates must name the appropriate school boards and coordinated transportation agencies as additional insureds. Individual school-board bid specifications may require higher limits—sometimes as much as a $5 million combined single limit (CSL).

What to do: Review the bid specifications, additional-insured names, vehicle inspections, driver records, and certificates before service begins or a contract renews.

Federal Child-Restraint Rule Updates School-Bus Labeling And Testing

A final National Highway Traffic Safety Administration rule took effect October 9, 2026. It exempts child-restraint systems designed for school buses from the side-impact testing requirements in FMVSS No. 213a, removes requirements for vehicle-anchorage attachments that generally do not match how school-bus restraints are installed, and updates required labels. The principal compliance dates are December 5 and December 8, 2026. This rule governs equipment manufacturers and certification; it does not change a transportation contractor's insurance limits.

What to do: When purchasing or installing school-bus child restraints, confirm that the product is labeled for school-bus use, follow the manufacturer's and school district's instructions, train staff, and document routine inspections. The rule does not require operators to discard an otherwise compliant restraint solely because the federal standards changed.

Workers' Compensation Coverage Remains Mandatory for Most Employers

New Jersey generally requires employers to maintain workers' compensation insurance or obtain approval to self-insure. The State warns that failure to insure can bring penalties of up to $5,000 for the first ten days and up to $5,000 for each additional ten-day period, along with direct liability for employee benefits after an injury.

What to do: Avoid coverage lapses, keep the required workplace notice posted, and report work injuries to the carrier promptly.

New Jersey's ABC-Test Rules Are Now Operative

NJDOL adopted final regulations clarifying the ABC test for worker classification. The rules became effective June 1 and operative October 1, 2026, and apply to New Jersey's unemployment compensation, wage-and-hour, and wage-payment laws. Receiving a 1099 instead of a W-2 does not, by itself, establish independent-contractor status. The hiring business must prove that all three parts of the ABC test are satisfied.

Part A: Freedom From Direction or Control

The worker must be, and remain, free from the employer's control or direction over the work under both the contract and the actual working relationship. Direction or control can include:

  • Giving instructions or training
  • Setting work hours
  • Specifying tools, supplies, or assistants
  • Defining responsibilities or the sequence of work
  • Having the right to fire the worker

If the employer exercises direction or control, Part A cannot be met.

Part B: Work Outside the Usual Business or Place of Business

The work must either be outside the employer's usual course of business or be performed outside all of the employer's places of business.

  • A fence installer working at a law office performs a service outside the law firm's usual business; a paralegal assisting its lawyers does not.
  • Facilities where physical or occupational therapists perform services through staffing contracts may be considered integral parts of the staffing business.

If the work is similar to the employer's usual business or is performed where that business ordinarily operates, Part B cannot be met.

Part C: Independently Established Business

The worker must customarily operate an independently established trade, occupation, profession, or business that can continue separately from the relationship with the employer. Relevant factors include:

  • The amount and duration of work for the current employer
  • Whether the worker employs others and pays their own expenses
  • Whether the worker serves more than one person or firm
  • Whether the worker uses their own tools, equipment, vehicles, or materials
  • The number and volume of other customers
  • How much work comes from the current employer compared with other customers for the same services

If a worker is misclassified: New Jersey remedies may include backpay and reimbursement for unlawful deductions, access to employment protections and benefits, a penalty of up to 5% of the worker's gross earnings during the prior 12 months, and correction of retaliatory actions. Employers should disclose employees and subcontracted labor accurately to their insurance agent or carrier and retain records supporting any independent-contractor classification.

What to do: Review who controls the work, where and how it is performed, and whether the worker operates a genuinely independent business. Keep written agreements and supporting records, report payroll and subcontracted labor accurately, and obtain legal or accounting guidance when a classification is uncertain.

NJCRIB Requests A 1.3% Workers' Compensation Rate Decrease For 2027

NJCRIB Circular 2527 reports that the Bureau filed for a 1.3% reduction in New Jersey's overall workers' compensation premium and rate level for policies effective January 1, 2027 or later. The proposal remains subject to approval by the New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance. If approved, it would be the twelfth consecutive year without an overall increase. An individual employer's premium may change by more or less because classification rates, payroll, experience modification, carrier pricing, credits, and other policy factors still apply.

What to do: Treat the 1.3% decrease as proposed until NJCRIB publishes approved rates and Manual changes. Employers should provide accurate payroll and classification information and review each renewal on its own figures rather than assuming every policy will decrease by 1.3%.

Prepare For New Jersey's 2027 Minimum Wage Increase

Effective January 1, 2027, New Jersey's minimum wage increases to $16.48 per hour for most workers and $16.25 per hour for workers at seasonal employers or small employers with fewer than six employees. Separate rates apply to agricultural, tipped, and long-term-care direct-care workers. These are announced 2027 rates, not proposed legislation.

What to do: Update wage and payroll budgets before January 1, and review estimated payroll on workers' compensation policies. A lower insurance rate alone does not guarantee a lower renewal or final audited premium.

Expanded Job-Protected Leave Requirements Are Now In Effect

Effective July 17, 2026, employees receiving New Jersey Temporary Disability or Family Leave Insurance benefits may have job protection when their leave is not already covered by the New Jersey Family Leave Act. This protection has no minimum employer-size or employee-work-history requirement. NJFLA coverage also expanded to employers with at least 15 employees, while employee eligibility was reduced to three months of employment and 250 hours worked during the preceding 12 months.

What to do: Review leave and reinstatement procedures, train managers to route requests promptly, and consult an employment professional before denying protected leave or reinstatement. Review employment-practices liability coverage, but remember that insurance does not replace legal compliance.

Report Employee Separations Within Seven Days

NJDOL's current instructions require employers to report employee separations through the Employer Response Portal within seven days of separation, without waiting for an unemployment claim. This includes layoffs and other departures. This is an existing unemployment-insurance compliance obligation, not a new 2026 insurance-coverage requirement.

What to do: Register with Employer Access, use the connected myNewJersey login to access the Employer Response Portal, and assign responsibility for timely submissions and monitoring claim notices. Keep copies of submitted information and respond promptly to follow-up requests.

Legislative Watch

A3396 Proposes a Lower Liability Minimum for Autocabs

Assembly Bill A3396 proposes reducing the minimum motor vehicle liability coverage required for autocabs from $1.5 million to $200,000. This is proposed legislation and does not itself change the current insurance requirement.

What to do: Taxi owners should continue meeting existing requirements and contract terms unless a change is enacted and becomes effective.
